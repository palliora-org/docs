---
title: "Shielded transactions"
---

A shielded transaction spends some notes and creates others. A zero-knowledge proof shows the transaction is valid. The pool checks the proof, and never sees the notes.

## Four kinds

| Kind         | What it does                                                                         | What becomes public                                 |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Deposit      | Moves tokens from your wallet into the pool and creates notes worth that amount.     | Your wallet, the token and the amount               |
| Transfer     | Spends your notes and creates notes for someone else. No tokens leave the pool.      | Only the fee token and amount                       |
| Withdraw     | Spends your notes and sends tokens to a public address.                              | The receiving address, the token and the amount     |
| Adaptor call | Spends your notes, sends tokens to an adaptor, and turns the result back into notes. | The adaptor, the tokens in and out, and the payload |

A deposit carries no fee. You pay its gas from your wallet. For the other kinds, the fee comes out of the transaction. See [Relaying and gas](/defi/learn/concepts/relaying-and-gas).

## Shapes

The circuit depends on how many notes a transaction spends and creates. The pool has one verifier per shape.

| Notes spent | Notes created |
| ----------- | ------------- |
| Up to 2     | 1, 2 or 3     |
| Up to 4     | 2 or 4        |
| Up to 8     | 2 or 4        |

The app picks the smallest shape that fits. Unused slots hold empty notes, with zero value and zero token id. The proof skips the tree check for them.

## What the proof shows

The proof is made on your device. It shows that:

1. Every spent note is in the commitment tree, and you know its contents.
2. You own each spent note, by a valid signature from your sign key over the transaction.
3. Each revealed nullifier is computed correctly from its note.
4. Each new commitment is computed correctly.
5. The value balances: notes spent, plus tokens put in, equals notes created, plus tokens taken out.
6. A seed for disclosure is encrypted correctly to the disclosure key. See [Accountable privacy and disclosure](/defi/learn/concepts/accountable-privacy-and-disclosure).
7. The account making the transaction is registered. Its root address is in the address tree, and the proof does not say which entry.

## What the pool checks

When a transaction arrives, the pool:

1. Checks that the commitment-tree root and the address-tree root are ones it knows.
2. Checks that no nullifier has been used before.
3. Checks the amounts and tokens that become public, and the deposit limits.
4. Checks the proof with the verifier for the transaction's shape.
5. Records the nullifiers, puts the new commitments in the queue, and moves any tokens. Only then is the transaction final.

On Ethereum the pool also screens the depositor against a sanctions list. Every value the circuit works with must be a valid field element, and a transaction with any value outside that range is rejected.
