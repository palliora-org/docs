**Free people. Safe societies.**

> *You are only free when no one is watching, and only safe when no one can hide behind that freedom.*

Privacy is fundamental to a free and secure society. It lets people speak, trade and work without being watched, and it lets businesses compete without giving away their plans. Accountability is just as fundamental. It is how a society stays safe, and how people can trust what they can't see.

For most of history, people have had to choose between the two. More privacy meant more room for crime. More safety meant more surveillance.

Cryptography lets us have both. Palliora is how the open internet gets both: private by default, accountable by design.

## Private by default

On Palliora, data is encrypted before it leaves its owner, and it stays encrypted in storage. It is decrypted only for the computation a contract allows, and only with the agreement of a quorum of independent Guardians. Traders keep their strategies to themselves. Businesses can work with sensitive data. People can use AI without handing over what they ask it.

## Accountable by design

Every computation runs under a Compute Contract whose terms are public and locked before anything executes. When there is a real reason to look, a specific record can be revealed through due process: a signed, publicly logged request, approved by independent Guardians, covering only what it names. No one holds a master key. Honest users are never exposed by default, and no one can hide behind their privacy forever. See [Accountable privacy](/learn/concepts/accountable-privacy).

## Where people already are

Palliora plugs into the chains and platforms applications already use. No one has to bridge assets, move liquidity or switch to a new chain. Privacy and accountability arrive where the users already are. See [Where Palliora runs](/learn/where-palliora-runs).

## It starts with finance and AI

Finance and AI are where private, accountable computation is needed most today.

* **Finance.** On-chain trading has grown to a scale where public order flow is a real cost to traders, and private trading already has proven demand.
* **AI.** Agents and models increasingly act on people's private data, and private AI services already have paying users. Palliora lets that work run privately and leaves a record anyone can check.
* **Regulation.** Regulators are signalling that privacy is acceptable when lawful disclosure is possible.
